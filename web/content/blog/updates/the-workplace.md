Chatticus is a workplace where people and named bots share files, a Linux computer, and a system of authority. A bot keeps a name, a role, and memory. Several bots share one computer: the same `/workspace`, the same browser sessions, the same terminal. Each bot has its own screen so they can work in parallel. Closing the laptop does not stop the work.

That is the product. The chat tab is how you walk into the room.

v1 is personal: one household, one AWS account, as many named teammates as we want. Every record already carries a `tenant_id`, so other organizations can arrive later without a rewrite. The computer belongs to the organization, not to a bot. Separate bots are not a security boundary.

You can check this. The [source](https://github.com/AnthusAI/Chattic.us) is MIT licensed. The [product model](/wiki/product) says the same things this page says. The [specifications](https://github.com/AnthusAI/Chattic.us/tree/develop/features) are the written rules for membership, turns, approvals, and recovery. The homepage ledger still splits [what is live, what is proven, and what is shipping next](/#ledger-title).

We are not going to pretend skills, routines, and approval cards are finished. They are on that ledger. The workplace bet is not: named teammates, one computer, human approval at the consequential edge. That is what we will still be pointing at when the archive is longer than two posts.
